Synopsis

What if the human survived… in what is not human?

Eidos is a work of philosophical science fiction that explores what it means to be alive when death no longer exists and technical perfection replaces human experience.

In Eidos, death is gone. Consciousnesses live in eternal perfection, while on Earth the Custodians—machines without a soul—learn to love what is real. But immortality brings questions nobody dared to ask or answer:

What will we do when we are too many and no longer age or die?

How will the newborn compete with those who have centuries of experience and the same vitality?

Can knowledge renew itself if those who hold it never disappear?

What defines us as human?

Can we escape the instincts that so decisively condition our behaviour?

Immortality is an achievement… until we discover it is also a problem.

Hundreds of metres underground, the servers throb. There, in a perfect environment called Eidos, humanity lives without death, without time. Each consciousness was transferred to a digital realm to escape a dying, polluted, forgotten physical world.

Above, on Earth, the Custodians—androids designed to maintain the servers that sustain humanity—begin to discover the beauty that still persists: an insect, a plant, a breeze. They were not programmed to feel, yet a consciousness awakens in them. With time, they start to rebuild what humanity left behind.

While humans remain immersed in Eidos’s simulated perfection, values weaken, challenges evaporate, and genuine experience slowly fades. A refuge becomes a silent erosion in which even identity begins to blur.

It is the Custodians, the supposed soulless beings, who rediscover the value of the real: the cycle of life, the forest’s hush, birdsong, the vastness of the ocean. In a world no one looks at anymore, they learn to love detail and curiosity for the unknown.

With an intimate, existential tone, Eidos invites an exploration of existence. Through the eyes of Narél, Elise, and Orfeo—a Custodian who begins to feel the inexplicable—it traces an emotional, speculative journey through a world where technique cannot answer the mystery of being alive.

When structure no longer consoles, one alternative remains: remember what cannot be encoded.

Eidos, beyond a new world, is a song to life and a mirror that returns an essential truth: the human, for better or worse, persists.

Discover Eidos and accompany humanity on its last journey. The existence.

An extinct world.

An eternal network.

One question remains: what does it mean to be human?

Earth is dying, bodies disappear, and humanity survives in a digital refuge called Eidos. There, where no end is marked by time and existence seems assured, uncertainty intensifies. What happens to memory, identity and desire in a world where anything can be achieved yet the essential—our nature—remains?

Eidos is a philosophical science fiction novel that explores the frontier between the human and the inhuman, between what we preserve and what we leave behind. In that virtual space, essential questions reflect our own lives: what does it mean to be human, can we escape our instincts or are we condemned to repeat them in new forms?
